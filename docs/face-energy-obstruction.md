# Why spectator-blind pair rules cannot transport one-face energy

The failure of the selected rule's additive face charges is not just a bad choice among
fourteen candidates. There is a finite-group theorem for this entire two-link mechanism.
Applied to our exhausted square-fiber census, it excludes every nonconstant real-valued
one-face energy for **all 5,730 nonidentity strict braid rules**.

This is a result about the specified microscopic dynamics, not a no-go theorem for emergent
energy, finite-group gauge theory, or the Wolfram Physics Project. In particular, it identifies
an assumption to change: the current operator writes links incident to four faces but chooses
its action from only two face holonomies.

## Assumptions and statement

Let $G$ be any finite group. Let a conjugation-equivariant pair map
$F:G^2\to G^2$ preserve the ordered product:

$$F(a,b)=(x,y),\qquad xy=ab.$$

Use the [shared-face patch and closing-link lift](shared-face-dynamics.md), with independent
rooted holonomies $A,B,C,D$. The operator reads the based pair
$(A,A^{-1}BA)$ and does not read $C,D$ when choosing its outputs. All exterior links stay fixed.
Require the same real- or rational-valued class function $q$ to give a conserved sum

$$E_q=\sum_f q(H_f)$$

for every connection and each allowed elementary update on this patch and its embeddings.
There are no restrictions to a seeded subgroup, a particular orbit, or a single small topology.

Define the normal subgroup generated by all first-component transport increments:

$$N_F=\left\langle x a^{-1}: (x,y)=F(a,b),\ (a,b)\in G^2\right\rangle^{\mathrm{normal}}.$$

**Theorem.** Such conserved sums are exactly those for which $q$ descends to a class function
on $G/N_F$. Every surviving $q(H_f)$ is unchanged **on each face separately**. In fact, the
entire quotient connection in $G/N_F$ is fixed link by link in a fixed frame convention.
These quantities cannot describe transfer of one-face energy between sites.

The proof does not require reversibility, involutivity, the braid relation, or a preferred
representation of $G$. Those additional conditions apply to the rule census, not to this theorem.

## Proof

Write $(X,Y)=F(A,A^{-1}BA)$ and $L=XA^{-1}$. Product preservation gives $XY=BA$, so
$AYA^{-1}=L^{-1}B$. The previously checked four-face update simplifies to

$$
(A,B,C,D)\longmapsto
\bigl(AL,\ L^{-1}B,\ L^{-1}C,\ B^{-1}LBD\bigr).
$$

Fix $A,B,D$ and vary $C$ freely. Conservation of the four-face sum forces

$$q(L^{-1}C)-q(C)=k(A,B,D)$$

independently of $C$. Summing over all $C\in G$ gives $|G|k=0$, because multiplication by
$L^{-1}$ permutes $G$. Over $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb Q$, $k=0$. Therefore $q$ is invariant
under left multiplication by every possible $L$. It is also invariant under their inverses
and products. Since $q$ is a class function, it is invariant under their conjugates as well.
As $(A,A^{-1}BA)$ ranges over all $G^2$, these increments generate precisely $N_F$.
Thus $q$ is constant on $N_F$ cosets and descends to a class function on $G/N_F$.

Conversely, if $q$ descends to that quotient, all four displayed terms are separately
unchanged: the left increments lie in $N_F$, and normality handles the right increment $L$
in $AL$ and the conjugated increment in $D$. This proves necessity and sufficiency on the patch.

For a general closing-link lift, its first oriented link is multiplied by $xa^{-1}\in N_F$.
The second increment in the common frame is

$$yb^{-1}=x^{-1}a=a^{-1}(xa^{-1})^{-1}a\in N_F.$$

Connector transport only conjugates this increment, and reversed edge orientation does not
change the quotient conclusion. Hence all quotient links are fixed. Every incident loop,
including loops beyond the four-face example, has the same quotient holonomy. This proves
sufficiency on arbitrary meshes using these lifts; the four independent spectator faces give
necessity for a universal law on a mesh family admitting that patch.

## Exact classification for the derived square fiber

The implementation reconstructs $G=\mathrm{Aut}(C_4)$ directly from square adjacency. For each
strict rule it independently checks product preservation, conjugation covariance, flat fixing,
involution, orientation reversal, and all $8^3$ braid triples, then computes $N_F$.

| Strict pair rules | $N_F$ | Dimension with $q(1)=0$ | Interpretation |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Identity only | $\{1\}$ | 4 | Every face class is fixed |
| All 5,730 nonidentity rules | $G$ | 0 | No nonconstant one-face sum survives |

The result covers all 3,592 strict subgroup-growing rules, not only the fourteen minimum-support
candidates. It does not enumerate every reversible law on $G^2$, every finite fiber, or every
larger-neighborhood rule.

An independent oracle solves the original four-holonomy conservation equations, without using
normal closure to obtain the nullspace. Across all 29 minimal symmetry-closure rules it checks
**118,784 configurations** and agrees with the theorem. These controls include nontrivial proper
normal subgroups: $|N_F|=2$ leaves three normalized face functions, while $|N_F|=4$ leaves one.
Those functions are locally frozen, not transported charges. Additional tests use cyclic groups,
$S_3$ with a nonzero identity index, and noninvertible product-preserving maps.

The theorem replaces an $|G|^4$ configuration search for this question with extraction of
$|G|^2$ increments followed by normal-subgroup closure. It also distinguishes an exactly frozen
quotient observable from a genuinely transported conserved density.

## What the theorem does not exclude

- Densities involving larger connected sets of loops or additional microscopic degrees of freedom.
- Rules whose decisions depend on all affected face data, rather than treating $C,D$ as spectators.
- Laws that conserve a quantity only after a specified complete update cycle, not after each gate.
- Restricted initial sectors, topology-specific invariants, nonlocal observables, and torsion-valued
  charges. The averaging proof explicitly uses characteristic zero.
- Geometry- or fiber-changing dynamics with a different state space and conservation equation.

The [neighboring-loop audit](observable-memory-and-invariants.md) separately excludes one common
28-coefficient pair-density ansatz for the selected rule; it is not a classification of all
larger-support energies.

## Relation to known physics and the next construction

Conserved additive observables in reversible cellular automata have long been used to investigate
statistical mechanics; this is not a new interpretation of energy. See
[Takesue's reversible-automaton study](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.59.2499).
Finite-group Hamiltonian gauge theories also have established constructions, including a
group-Laplacian electric operator; see
[Mariani, Pradhan, and Ercolessi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12224). Neither gauge symmetry nor
reversibility selects such a Hamiltonian for our combinatorial model.

Our next primitive search should let an update read its full affected neighborhood, enumerate
boundary-preserving gauge-covariant reversible moves, and **derive** their conservation laws.
The search must not choose a familiar plaquette energy first and reject moves until it is
conserved. Persistent excitations, response, and size/schedule controls follow only after a
nontrivial law survives embedding tests. A larger simulation of the excluded pair family does
not resolve this particular obstruction.

We have not established priority for the theorem in the literature. The contribution here is
the explicit proof for this lift, its complete finite-census application, and reproducible
counterexample/control machinery—not a claim to have derived physical energy or matter.
