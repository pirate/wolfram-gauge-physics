# Adjacency-assisted curvature motion escapes the fixed-subgroup trap

A new candidate primitive uses **ambient fiber adjacency**, not just words
in the existing loop transports. It moves a nonidentity rotation by one
adjacency-derived step and adjusts a neighboring reflection to keep the
ordered boundary product fixed.

It is reversible, gauge covariant, and orientation covariant, and it
preserves the existing reflection-length charge. On actual shared links
it can grow an embedded six-element holonomy image to the full
$18$-, $54$-, or $162$-element group. Only one base edge changes.

The isolated pair dynamics has an exact path-graph generator and a
diffusive angular scaling limit. Coupling it to one existing reaction
gives an exactly solvable process with an internal waiting state and a
derived memory term. Neither a heat equation nor a Hamiltonian is used
to update the links.

This remains a **chosen microscopic model on supplied cycle fibers and
a supplied triangular base mesh**. It does not derive changing fiber
graphs, spacetime, physical energy, quantum amplitudes, or matter. Its
continuum coordinate is an internal curvature angle, not physical space.

## Derive an elementary transport step from the fiber graph

Let $n=2m+1\ge3$ and derive $G=\operatorname{Aut}(C_n)$ from adjacency.
Exactly two automorphisms move every vertex to a neighboring vertex.
They are mutual inverses. Call this unordered, graph-defined pair $S$.
No orientation is selected as part of the rule.

For a nonidentity rotation $z$, all fiber vertices have the same
displacement distance $d(z)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$. There is exactly one
$u(z)\in S$ such that

$$u(z)^{d(z)}=z.$$

This extracts an oriented elementary step from the input rotation using
the ambient graph. It need not belong to the subgroup generated by the
input tuple. For example, a three-step rotation on $C_9$ generates only
three rotations, whereas its adjacency-derived unit step generates nine.

Under a fiber-frame automorphism $g$, adjacency and graph distance are
preserved. Uniqueness therefore gives

$$u(gzg^{-1})=g\,u(z)\,g^{-1}.$$

Thus using this extra graph structure does not break gauge covariance.
It does go beyond multiplication and inversion of the available transports,
which explains how it escapes the [word-map subgroup restriction](fiber-word-conservation.md).

This also explains why the old exact refinement intertwining need not
survive: an elementary coarse step embeds as several fine steps, not as
one fine adjacency step. The new rule deliberately uses the finer graph
information. It activates directions already present in that supplied
fiber; it does not create the finer fiber itself.

## The angular involutions

For $j=1,\ldots,m-1$, define $A_j$ to exchange rotation magnitudes $j$
and $j+1$, retaining the orientation selected by $u(z)$:

$$A_j(z)=
\begin{cases}
u(z)^{j+1},&d(z)=j,\\
u(z)^j,&d(z)=j+1,\\
z,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}$$

There is one additional central involution,

$$A_m(z)=\begin{cases}z^{-1},&d(z)=m,\\z,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}$$

The central exchange is also an elementary step: on a cycle of length
$2m+1$, rotations by $m$ and $-m$ differ by one vertex step. Including it
keeps the two orientations connected; silently dropping it would alter
the framed dynamics.

These maps are involutions, commute with inversion and conjugation, and
every changing move satisfies $z^{-1}A_j(z)\in S$. They are precisely
the inversion-symmetric edge exchanges of the nonidentity rotation path.

For a based pair containing one reflection and one nonidentity rotation,
let $P$ be its ordered product. Set

$$T_j(R,z)=\bigl(PA_j(z)^{-1},A_j(z)\bigr),\qquad P=Rz,$$

$$T_j(z,R)=\bigl(A_j(z),A_j(z)^{-1}P\bigr),\qquad P=zR.$$

Fix all other pairs. The adjusted entry is again a reflection. The maps
preserve $P$, are involutions, and commute with simultaneous conjugation
and reversed inversion. Shared-edge reconstruction realizes the pair
target while fixing every exterior link and every other face.

These are local changes of one base edge and nearest-neighbor changes
in the rotation-action graph. Computing $u(z)$ uses the ambient fiber
structure; this is **not yet a derivation from a fixed-size local rewrite
of the fiber graph itself**.

## Conservation and the structural choices behind it

The angular moves preserve reflection count and nonidentity-rotation
count separately. Since the rotation-class path is connected, their
complete normalized additive class-charge space has two dimensions:
an arbitrary common reflection value and an arbitrary common nonidentity
rotation value.

Adding the existing reaction $RRR\leftrightarrow ERZ$ gives the single
relation $q(Z)=2q(R)$. Hence the augmented bank still conserves exactly
the same one-dimensional class-charge family,

$$q(e)=0,\qquad q(R)=c,\qquad q(Z)=2c.$$

No charge or spectral cost is evaluated in constructing the angular maps.
However, selecting mixed reflection/rotation pairs and excluding identity
are explicit structural choices. They are why the conservation survives;
adjacency alone does not force those choices. The spectral observable
$2-\operatorname{tr}U(g)$ is generally **not** conserved by these moves.

## Actual subgroup growth without changing the boundary

Choose coordinates only to describe a witness, and write
$t_k(v)=v+k$, $r_a(v)=a-v$. For $n=3^a$, $a\ge2$, set $k=n/3$.
The pair

$$(r_k,t_k),\qquad r_kt_k=r_0,$$

generates a six-element dihedral subgroup. A single angular step gives

$$\boxed{(r_k,t_k)\longmapsto(r_{k-1},t_{k-1}).}$$

The boundary remains $r_0$. Since $k-1$ is coprime to $n$, the new
rotation generates the full rotation subgroup and the pair generates
all of $\operatorname{Aut}(C_n)$.

On the side-four shared-link mesh, the witnesses have whole-mesh charge
$4$ before and after. The active pair contributes $3$; a fixed exterior
reflection contributes $1$. The third face of the fan remains identity.
One internal edge changes, all exterior links stay fixed, and the inverse
returns the exact original raw connection. Rooted holonomy image is
computed from spanning-tree transports of all graph edges, not inferred
only from face labels:

| Fiber | Initial image order | Final image order |
|---|---:|---:|
| $C_9$ | 6 | 18 |
| $C_{27}$ | 6 | 54 |
| $C_{81}$ | 6 | 162 |

The algebraic increase in subgroup order is not an increase of physical
energy or a macroscopic field jump. The rotation changes by just one
fiber step, of dimensionless angle $2\pi/n$. These are nonflat seeded
states; the construction does not create excitations from the flat vacuum.

## Exact isolated-pair dynamics

Fix the boundary reflection $P$, the pair order $(R,Z)$, and the exterior
links. The pair is determined by its nonidentity rotation $z$.
Choose either elementary step to label rotations $z=t_k$, $1\le k<n$.
This is a reporting coordinate, not a preferred orientation in the rules.

The $m$ involutions connect these states as the path

$$t_1\;\text{---}\;t_2\;\text{---}\;\cdots\;\text{---}\;t_{n-1}.$$

Let $L$ be its combinatorial Laplacian: endpoints have degree one,
interior points degree two, and adjacent off-diagonal entries are $-1$.
Independent rate-one clocks for the involutions give generator $-L$.
Uniformly proposing one of the $m$ involutions instead gives transition
matrix $I-L/m$, including idle proposals. These are explicit clock choices.

For $N=n-1=2m$, the eigenfunctions and decay rates are

$$f_\ell(k)=\cos\left(\frac{\pi\ell(k-1/2)}N\right),\qquad
\lambda_\ell=2-2\cos\left(\frac{\pi\ell}N\right),
\quad0\le\ell<N.$$

After $t$ proposals from $k_0$, the exact mean is

$$\mathbb E[f_\ell(k_t)]
=f_\ell(k_0)(1-\lambda_\ell/m)^t.$$

Conjugation by the boundary reflection sends $k\mapsto n-k$.
Even $\ell$ are invariant under this remaining conjugation and descend
to rotation-class observables. Odd $\ell$ change sign: they describe
framed coordinates, not gauge-invariant scalar observables. Quotienting
the isolated pair folds the path to the $m$ rotation classes. The central
exchange is then invisible, although it remains present in raw framed
trajectories.

The sampled runs used 128 independent trajectories each on $C_9,C_{27},C_{81}$,
starting at $k_0=n/3$ and updating actual shared links. They total
1,765,248 proposals. Across the recorded nonzero-time checkpoints, the
largest discrepancy from the exact mode mean is 1.74 estimated standard
errors. Errors use independent trajectories, not correlated time samples.

The first nonidle angular event already grows the holonomy image to the
full group. Its waiting time is geometric with success probability $2/m$.
Measured means are $2.063\pm0.135$, $6.398\pm0.523$, and
$21.305\pm1.699$ proposals, versus exact means $2$, $6.5$, and $20$.
No trajectories were censored.

## The angular continuum limit, and what it is not

With $h=2\pi/n$ and scaled time $\tau=h^2t/m$ for the proposal clock,

$$\lambda_\ell/h^2\longrightarrow\ell^2/4.$$

The resulting generator is the second derivative on the internal angular
interval $(0,2\pi)$ with reflecting endpoints. Gauge-invariant modes
fold it to $(0,\pi)$ with reflecting endpoints. This follows from the
path generator and its exact eigenmodes; no differential equation enters
the primitive update rule.

The scaled lowest gauge-even rates are $1.20189$, $1.07316$, and $1.02463$
at $n=9,27,81$, approaching $1$. The frame-odd rates approach $1/4$.
These are rates in a dimensionless internal coordinate under a chosen
rescaling, not calibrated physical time or spacetime dimensions.

The interval is cut at identity because the primitives forbid a rotation
from becoming identity. It is not unrestricted diffusion on a circle:
the endpoints reflect instead of joining. That boundary behavior is a
consequence of the microscopic type restriction and must not be hidden
when comparing with continuum gauge physics. This is classical angular
diffusion, not a derived Maxwell or Schrödinger equation.

## Autonomous activation from three coarse reflections

The finer directions need not be activated by manually inserting a
rotation first. Use the existing reaction rule 27 on the three-face fan,
and the $m$ angular gates on its last two based loops. Choose one of these
$m+1$ channels uniformly on each proposal.

For a fixed boundary reflection $P$, define

$$X_z=(zP,zP,P),\qquad Y_z=(e,zP,z),\qquad z\ne e.$$

The existing reaction exchanges $X_z\leftrightarrow Y_z$. Angular moves
act on $Y_z$ only, changing $z$ along the path. Starting at $X_{t_{n/3}}$
there are three reflection faces and no rotation face in the active fan.
An autonomous reaction creates the rotation, and an angular move accesses
the fine directions. Every step preserves the same total charge.

The resulting component has $2N=2(n-1)$ framed states. Recovering its
generator directly from the actual primitives gives

$$L_{\mathrm{coupled}}=
\begin{pmatrix}I&-I\\-I&I+L\end{pmatrix}.$$

Each $Y_z$ is a vertex on the angular path, with one attached $X_z$
waiting state. For a path eigenvalue $\lambda$, its two decay rates are

$$\boxed{\mu_\pm(\lambda)=
\frac{\lambda+2\pm\sqrt{\lambda^2+4}}2.}$$

At long wavelengths, $\mu_-=\lambda/2+O(\lambda^2)$: in this isolated
equal-rate model, the curvature spends half its equilibrium time in the
three-reflection configuration, where angular motion pauses. The
effective angular diffusion rate is therefore halved. This factor is
not asserted for the full twelve-reaction bank on an extended mesh.

The angular marginal is not itself Markovian. For mode populations
$x,y$ in the two configurations, let $u=x+y$. In the per-channel clock,

$$\dot x=y-x,\qquad\dot y=x-(1+\lambda)y,$$

so eliminating the internal state gives

$$\ddot u+(2+\lambda)\dot u+\lambda u=0.$$

Both decay rates are real and nonnegative. The second time derivative
encodes waiting-state memory, **not** an oscillating wave or quantum
amplitude. An initially all-reflection mode has $\dot u(0)=0$ because
a reaction must occur before angular motion can begin.

The mean first full-subgroup activation time from $X_{t_{n/3}}$ is exactly
$2(m+1)$ proposals. One reaction channel is active in $X$; in the initial
$Y$ state there are two angular exits and one return reaction. The two
elementary first-step equations give the stated mean without fitting.
Across 128 independent autonomous runs per fiber, the measured means are
$11.594\pm0.803$, $28.859\pm2.169$, and $79.055\pm5.753$, versus
$10$, $28$, and $82$. Raw event sequences and connections are saved.

## Status and limitations

This is a concrete mechanism for accessing finer transport directions
without losing gauge covariance, boundary transport, or the existing
coarse charge. It adds ambient-adjacency information that the old bank
could not use, and it supports a mathematically controlled internal
diffusion limit with an explicit reaction-memory correction.

The angular gates are exercised in this scientific apparatus; they have
not silently replaced the default evolution bank. The selection of a
mixed pair, exclusion of identity, and equal-rate stochastic scheduling
remain model choices. The fiber geometry and base geometry are supplied,
and the generator is a classical Markov generator, not a physical
Hamiltonian. Interactions among many overlapping active pairs, spatial
scaling, and actual fiber-changing rewrites remain unresolved.

The [full local-bank calculation](fiber-collective-modes.md) now restores
all twelve reactions and both adjacent supports. Its larger reflection
configuration space introduces additional subgroup memory; the isolated
factor-of-two diffusion correction does not describe that full component.
The follow-up derives a vanishing-gap bound and compares exact microscopic
first-exit predictions with raw-link trajectories.

Path random walks and their spectral analysis are established mathematics;
see Levin and Peres, [Markov Chains and Mixing Times](https://pages.uoregon.edu/dlevin/MARKOV/).
Finite-group quantum gauge Hamiltonians are a distinct construction; see
[Hamiltonians and gauge-invariant Hilbert space for lattice Yang-Mills-like theories with finite gauge group](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12224).
We have not supplied that Hilbert space or Hamiltonian here, and no
independent novelty review of this application has been completed.
